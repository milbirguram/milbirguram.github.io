Make an explanation earn its keep

A strong pattern in the weights is a place to start looking. It is not yet an explanation. Remove that pattern, preserve everything else, and see what changes. Then compare it with a random change of the same size.

Try another initialisation. The same task can produce different dependencies. These models sometimes improve after an intervention; that is part of the result.

What was trained, and what this can establish

Three small networks were trained from random weights for 18,000 updates on addition modulo 31. Each has 3,968 learned parameters: a 31 × 64 embedding E and a 64 × 31 readout U. Its computation is h = (E[a] + E[b])², then logits = hU. Sharing embeddings makes commutativity an architectural assumption. No trigonometric features are supplied during training.

Of 496 unordered input pairs, 396 train the network and 100 are excluded from gradient updates. Reversed pairs stay in the same group. Architecture and settings were chosen after exploratory pilot runs, so these 100 sums are unseen during training, not an untouched confirmatory test set. The curves show all 25 saved checkpoints, including regressions.

The bands are sine/cosine pairs from a discrete Fourier transform of the input embeddings. Removing one preserves the column means and the readout. Keeping one retains that band and the means. The control subtracts a repeatable random change with the same total squared magnitude, projected outside the selected band and the constant component. It is one control direction, not a statistical significance test. Every displayed prediction and intervention score is recomputed from the shipped weights.

These are purpose-built quadratic networks inspired by interpretability research. They are not the transformers in the papers below, and a spectrum does not establish that they implement a particular Clock or Pizza algorithm. Nor is their short training lag a reproduction of a long grokking plateau.
