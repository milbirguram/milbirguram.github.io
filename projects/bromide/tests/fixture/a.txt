CHAPTER I.

The morning was cold and grey, and the road to the mill was wet with rain. Mr. Hale walked along the road with his hands in his pockets. He did not look up at the sky. He knew that the sky would be grey, and he knew that the rain would come again before noon.

At the mill the water was high. The wheel turned slowly, and the old miller stood at the door. "Good morning," said the miller. "Good morning," said Mr. Hale. They stood for a while and said nothing more, for there was nothing more to say.

It was a cold and dark night when Mr. Hale came home. The wind blew through the trees, and the rain beat upon the windows. His wife sat by the fire with her work in her lap. She did not look up when he came in, but she knew that he was tired.

"You are late," she said at last.

"The road was bad," he said. "The water is high at the mill, and the wheel will not turn much longer if the rain does not stop."

She put down her work and looked at the fire. The fire was low, and the room was cold. She rose and put another log upon the fire, and the light came back into the room.

CHAPTER II.

In the morning the rain had stopped. The sun rose over the hills, and the road to the mill was bright with water. Mr. Hale walked along the road again. The birds were singing in the trees, and the air was clean and cold.

The miller was at the door. "The water is going down," he said. "The wheel will turn today." Mr. Hale nodded. He was glad of it, though he did not say so. He was not a man who said much.

They went into the mill together. It was dark inside, and the air was full of dust. The great stones turned slowly. The miller put his hand upon the wall and listened to the sound of the wheel. "She is a good old mill," he said, "and she will last longer than either of us."
