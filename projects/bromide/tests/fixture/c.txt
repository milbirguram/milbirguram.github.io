ON SMALL THINGS.

A man who would understand the world must first attend to small things. The large things are easy to see, and every man sees them; but the small things are seen only by those who look for them. It is in the small things that the truth is to be found.

Consider the kettle on the fire. It is a small thing, and no man thinks about it. Yet the kettle ticked as it cooled, and in that sound there is a lesson about heat and time. The world is full of such lessons, if a man will only listen.

[Illustration: A kettle upon a fire.]

It is not the business of the essayist to say what is new. It is his business to say what is true, and to say it plainly. Most of what is true has been said before, and most of what is new is not true. The essayist must therefore be content to say old things in a plain way.

Let us consider, then, the small things of the day. The morning is cold, and the fire is low. The tea is hot, and the bread is fresh. The letter has come, and the news is good. These are small things, and yet a life is made of them.

A man who would be happy must attend to small things. The large things are not in his power; the small things are. He cannot stop the rain, but he can put another log upon the fire. He cannot make the sun rise, but he can be awake when it does.
