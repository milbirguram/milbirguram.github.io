My dear sister,

The sea has been calm all week, and I have walked along the shore every morning. The sun rose over the water each day like a slow lamp, and the gulls sat upon the rocks and said nothing at all. I think of you often, and of the house, and of the garden in the rain.

The village is small. There is one shop, one church, and one road that runs down to the harbour. The fishermen go out before the sun rises and come home when the sun is high. They do not talk much, but they are kind, and they have been very good to me.

Yesterday the wind rose in the afternoon, and by evening the sea was white. It was a cold and dark night, and the wind blew through the trees behind the house until the morning. I did not sleep. I sat by the fire and read your letter again, and I was glad of it.

Write to me soon, my dear sister, and tell me how the garden does. Tell me if the roses have come, and whether the old tree by the gate is still standing.

Your loving brother.

My dear sister,

Your letter came this morning, and I read it by the window while the rain fell upon the harbour. I am glad the roses have come. I am glad the old tree is still standing. The sea is grey today, and the boats have not gone out.

I have been thinking about the house. When I come home in the spring, we shall walk in the garden together, and you shall show me everything. The sun will be warm, and the rain will have stopped, and we shall not speak of the winter at all.

Your loving brother.
