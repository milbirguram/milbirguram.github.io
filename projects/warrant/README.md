# Warrant

A model predicts delivery times and promises that, for a chosen share of new trips, the true time falls inside its band. The page lets you watch that promise hold, break it by shifting the data, and try three repairs. The card preview shows the promise holding, the trips shifting, and coverage recovering once ACI with recalibration takes over (captioned “recalibrate and adapt”, since the recent window does most of that work). Page: `projects/warrant/index.html`. Model: `model.js` (browser and Node). Tests: `tests/model.test.js`.

## What it’s for

A conformal prediction band comes with a real guarantee, and it is easy to forget what the guarantee stands on. The page is meant to leave a visitor knowing that the promise of split conformal prediction rests on exchangeability, that the new data must look, statistically, like the calibration data, and knowing how to tell when that has stopped being true: coverage falling out of the range it should wander in, and new inputs that a classifier can tell apart from the old ones. It then shows what three repairs fix, and what each one does not.

## How I know

**What is modelled.** Delivery distance is log-normal (median 2.5 km, log-sd 0.5). Time in minutes is 8 + 3x + 0.15x² plus normal noise whose standard deviation is 1.5 + 0.4x, so long trips are both slower per km and less predictable. The base model is an ordinary least-squares straight line fitted on 300 trips: deliberately a little wrong, as real models are. Split conformal prediction calibrates on 500 further trips. The score is the absolute error, and the band’s half-width is the k-th smallest score with k = ⌈(n + 1)(1 − α)⌉, which is the empirical quantile at level ⌈(n + 1)(1 − α)⌉/n (Vovk, Gammerman and Shafer; Lei et al.). If k > n the band is unbounded.

New trips stream in one at a time. Three controls change the world they come from, and each change closes 2% of the remaining gap per trip, so shifts arrive gradually:

- **Typical distance** (covariate shift): the median distance moves up to 4.5 km. The rule from distance to time is unchanged.
- **Long trips noisier**: the noise sd grows by up to 0.8 more minutes per km. The page reports this as the real change in spread at 5 km (up to ×2.1); at 2.5 km it is ×1.8 and at 10 km ×2.5.
- **Each km takes longer** (concept drift): up to 2 more minutes per km.

Every method sees the same stream, so they can be compared trip by trip:

- **Weighted conformal** (Tibshirani, Barber, Candès and Ramdas 2019). Each calibration score gets weight w(Xᵢ), the test point gets w(x) with its score placed at +∞, and the band uses the 1 − α quantile of that weighted distribution. With the *true* ratio, w is the exact ratio of the two log-normal densities, known only because the world is simulated. With the *estimated* ratio, w is the odds from a logistic regression on (1, ln x, (ln x)²) that separates the 500 calibration distances from the latest 300 new ones, refitted every 20 trips. The same classifier’s in-sample AUC is shown as the “input check”.
- **Adaptive conformal inference** (Gibbs and Candès 2021). After each trip, αₜ₊₁ = αₜ + γ(α − errₜ), and the band uses the conformal quantile at level 1 − αₜ. When αₜ ≤ 0 the band is unbounded, and when αₜ ≥ 1 it is empty, which is what their bound requires. The page offers two versions, each with its own αₜ. *ACI alone* (the default) ranks each new error against the fixed calibration errors. *ACI with recalibration* ranks it against the latest 300 labelled trips (the window starts as the last 300 calibration trips), as in their own experiments, where the scores are re-estimated on recent data. A third, hidden method, *recalibration alone*, uses that same window at the fixed level 1 − α (γ = 0); it is drawn as a dotted line whenever ACI with recalibration is shown.

  The two parts of ACI with recalibration do different jobs, and the page says so. In a 4000-trip run with full concept drift at trip 500 (seed 5), the steady-state miss rate after trip 1500 was 10.04% with αₜ and 9.96% without it, with median widths of 19.5 and 19.2 minutes, and αₜ settled back near α. So the window supplies the steady state. αₜ protects the transition: the lowest 200-trip coverage between trips 500 and 1000 was 86% with it and 69% without. ACI alone also reaches a 10% miss rate, but under that drift its band was unbounded 42% of the time (74% with all three shifts at once): that is the width its guarantee costs when the scores no longer describe the world.
- **Conformalised quantile regression** (Romano, Patterson and Candès 2019). Linear quantile lines at α/2 and 1 − α/2, fitted on the training trips by the MM algorithm of Hunter and Lange (2000), then widened or narrowed by the conformal quantile of max(q_lo − y, y − q_hi) on the calibration trips. Under the page’s covariate shift CQR happens to hold up (88.1% over 200 calibration draws, against split’s 77.8%): its lines roughly track the conditional 5% and 95% quantiles at every distance, because here the noise sd is linear in distance and the mean is nearly so, and a band with roughly the right coverage at every distance does not care which distances occur. Nothing in its guarantee promises that, and under concept drift it covers 40%. The page’s CQR text changes with the shift to say this.

The shaded “expected if nothing changed” range is not a normal approximation. Under exchangeability, split conformal’s coverage given its calibration set is Beta(k, n + 1 − k) with k = n + 1 − ⌊(n + 1)α⌋ (Vovk 2012; Angelopoulos and Bates 2021, in their section on the effect of the size of the calibration set), so the number of covered trips among W new ones is beta-binomial. The range is that distribution’s central 95%, computed exactly for each window size. It is a 95% range, so even with nothing changed about one window in twenty falls outside it (2.1% of 20,000 windows at the default seed, which is under 5% because the range also covers the variation between calibration sets); the step-1 text says so, and changes when the coverage is outside the range.

**What is simplified.** One input, not several. Synthetic data from a world the page knows, so the true likelihood ratio is available, which it never is in practice. Noise is normal and symmetric. The base model is never refitted. Trip outcomes are revealed immediately after each prediction, which ACI depends on and which real deliveries would roughly allow but many applications do not. The scatter’s axes are fixed (0 to 12 km, 0 to 90 min); rare points beyond them are drawn on the edge.

**What I checked, and how.** `node projects/warrant/tests/model.test.js` (about 30 seconds, 20 checks, all seeded). On the last run:

- (a) Exchangeable coverage, 4000 repetitions each, fresh training, calibration and test data in each: n = 24, α = 0.1 gave 91.88% (exact theory 92.00%, allowed [90.00%, 94.00%]); n = 12, α = 0.2 gave 84.49% (exact 84.62%, allowed [80.00%, 87.69%]); n = 33, α = 0.05 gave 97.03% (exact 97.06%, allowed [95.00%, 97.94%]). These n were chosen so (n + 1)(1 − α) is not a whole number, which puts the exact value strictly inside the interval. The page’s own simulator at its own settings (n = 500, α = 0.1, 2000 calibration draws) gave 90.10% against an exact 90.02%. The beta-binomial range held all but 4.6% of 1500 independent windows of 200 trips.
- (b) The quantile function equals a naive “smallest score whose empirical CDF reaches ⌈(n + 1)(1 − α)⌉/n” across ten n and six α, with worked cases including the floating-point trap 20 × 0.9 and the unbounded case n = 5, α = 0.1.
- (c) With the median distance moved from 2.5 to 4.5 km (400 calibration draws × 150 trips), split conformal covered 77.53%, short of the promise by 12.48 points. Weighted conformal with the true ratio covered 90.93%, with 0.27% of bands unbounded. With the estimated ratio it covered 91.86% against split’s 77.63%.
- (d) For both ACI versions, the running miss rate stayed within Gibbs and Candès’s bound (max(α₁, 1 − α₁) + γ)/(γT) at every T in five 6000-trip scenarios (no shift; covariate shift; drift with γ = 0.005; noisier long trips with γ = 0.05; all three at once, abruptly, with γ = 0.1). Final miss rates were between 9.93% and 10.00%, while split missed up to 36%. A separate check pins down the decomposition described above (steady-state miss rates and widths of ACI with recalibration and recalibration alone within 1.5 points and 5%; ACI alone unbounded more than 20% of the time under drift; αₜ lifting the transition trough by more than 5 points).
- (e) A static check that the stage in `index.html` has no typed-in percentages, minutes, kilometres or trip counts, that no slider has its range in the HTML, and that `app.js` carries no target coverage, z-score, quantile rule or model constants. Every number on the page is computed by `model.js` from the simulated data.
- Also: the quantile lines put 5%, 50% and 95% of 2000 trips below them (within half a point); CQR covers 89.83% under exchangeability, 88.08% under the covariate shift and 40.00% under full drift; the input check reads 0.52 without shift and 0.81 with it; concept drift leaves the input check near 0.5 while coverage collapses; and the default seed is typical (see below).

I checked the page in a browser at desktop width and at 375 × 812, the card preview at 320 × 200, and the projects grid, with no console errors. Below 880 px the shift and repair controls move to just under the scatter, led by a small coverage readout, so a slider and its effect are on screen together.

**The look.** The stage is styled as a bond certificate: intaglio green and ink on security paper, a guilloché border (`frame.svg`), Playfair Display and Playfair Display SC (self-hosted under `fonts/`, SIL OFL, licence in `fonts/OFL.txt`). The band is drawn as an engraved ribbon: its hatching runs with the band, so the lines spread where the band widens. The seal on the scatter (`engraving.js`, linework computed from polar curves) carries one signal and no other: it cracks while the windowed coverage sits below the range expected if nothing had changed, the same test that drives the “below the expected range” readout, and shows a mended seam once coverage is back. It can crack by chance with nothing changed, as the readout can. Its centre shows the promised coverage, 1 − α, computed from the model. Restyling changed no model code, no copy and no tests.

**Things a careful reader should know.** Weighted conformal’s guarantee is on average over calibration sets. For a single calibration set its coverage under the full shift varies a lot: across 60 seeds it ranged from 80% to 100%, because only about 50 to 190 of the 500 calibration trips effectively count. The default seed (63) was chosen because it is typical for every method at once, not because it flatters one: over 120 seeds the averages were split 95.1% on short trips and 84.6% on long ones, CQR 89.6% and 90.0%, split 77.1% and weighted 91.0% under the full covariate shift, and seed 63 gives 95.8%, 85.8%, 90.5%, 90.8%, 77.7% and 91.3%. (An earlier default, seed 5, was typical for weighted conformal but reversed CQR’s short/long gap, 85% against 93%.) “New past data” draws another. Split conformal’s coverage is marginal: even with no shift, trips longer than the calibration median are covered less often than short ones, because the noise grows with distance. CQR closes that gap on average over calibration draws; for any one draw the balance varies. ACI’s guarantee is about the long-run average only; it can be wide or unbounded for stretches, and it says nothing about any one trip.

**What would change the conclusion.** If new trips were exchangeable with the calibration trips, none of the repairs would be needed, and the plain band would keep its promise; the page shows that case first. If the shift were in how distance turns into time rather than in which distances occur, weighted conformal would not help at all, and only a method that watches outcomes, like ACI, would. If outcomes were never revealed, ACI could not run, and only the input check would give any warning, and only of covariate shift.
