Change what counts as evidence

Try Each person’s click rate, then increase exploration. Switch to a population with a shared favourite. Concentrated attention can be a good match when people actually want the same thing.

The question is whether a feed is serving people’s interests, or merely measuring the opportunity it gave them to click. Both worlds make one recommendation per person each day. World II learns from a separate, randomly assigned observation instead of that recommendation. Those probes would have a real cost outside this experiment.

Inside the experiment

What is held fixed

Preferences never change. Each person has a fixed probability of clicking each category. Every day’s recommendation is selected using the previous day’s evidence. The two worlds share the same people and the same random draws for recommendation outcomes. Only their training observations differ.

Total clicks ranks cumulative click counts. Click rate uses a Beta(1,3) prior; personal ranking keeps one estimate per person and category. Exploration recommends a uniformly random category. Three initial observations per person slightly favour the first category. These are deliberately small algorithms, not a model of a commercial platform.

Expected click chance is the average of the simulation’s true click probabilities for the chosen recommendations, not a measurement of human wellbeing. Breadth is exp(entropy) of the day’s exposure shares: 1 means one category, 6 means an even spread. The plotted curves are computed here, not scripted outcomes.

Independent probes can improve the evidence while also giving up feedback from useful recommendations. More diversity is not automatically better, and these simulated results do not establish how real people respond.
