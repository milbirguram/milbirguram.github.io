    What this map can’t tell you

    1. Assessed is not sold. Each roll is BC Assessment’s estimate of market value on 1 July of the year before. It is not a sale price, and a rise in tax bills is not a rise in value.
    2. Where, not why. The map shows where medians moved. Nothing here measures migration, rates, zoning or anything else that might explain it.
    3. Strata kept apart. A strata unit carries its own share of the land under its building. Units are counted one by one and never pooled with lots or added up, so no land is counted twice.
    4. Lots are not only houses. They include shops, offices and industrial land, which is why Downtown leads. The older rolls do not say which is which.
    5. Placed by today’s parcels. Properties are put in an area through today’s parcel map. Up to 3% of older records match no current parcel; they count in the whole-city figures only.
    6. Medians move when the mix moves. A new tower of small units can pull an area’s median down without any home losing value.
    7. Withheld. Areas with fewer than 10 properties in a view are hatched and left blank.
